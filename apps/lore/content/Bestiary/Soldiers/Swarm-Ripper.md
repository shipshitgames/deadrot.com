# Swarm Ripper

**At a glance:** [[Scourge]] melee fodder · the most common thing in the war · a [[Scourge-Host-Families|Rot-Infested Flesh Host]] with blade forearms and one instruction: close the distance · does not coordinate, cannot relay, cannot think outside [[CANON|Choir]] range · dies cheap and is worth nothing alone, until there are enough of them to matter — and there are always enough of them · the density that lets the [[CANON|Choir]] function · appears in [[Scourge-Survivors]] / [[Deadlane]] / [[Redline]].

The first thing you kill is a Ripper. The ten-thousandth thing you kill is a Ripper.

They are what the [[Scourge]] makes out of whatever it finds when it is not spending resources on
something difficult. A conquered body — humanoid, animal, something that used to be neither — cracked
open at the sternum, parasite tissue laced through the muscles and spine, the hands grown into blades of
fused bone and black chitin, and the thing that used to understand need and loss reduced to one operating
instruction: *close the distance*. Whatever the host was before [[Zero-Day]], it is not that anymore. It
is the most basic expression of what the [[Scourge]] is ([[CANON]] §1): a host reshaped until it fits
the need.

A single Ripper is easy to kill. The [[Scourge]] designed it that way. It does not invest in quality at
the bottom of its order of battle — it invests in volume. A Ripper costs almost nothing: cheap
conversion, cheap kit, no relay, no special growth. The [[CANON|Choir]] tells it where to go and it
goes; outside Choir range it is feral, blind, and dumb, the most primitive kind of dangerous there is.
The parasite grows Rippers the way a body grows white cells — constantly, automatically, in numbers that
exhaust the imagination — and it loses them the same way, without counting and without slowing. It
consumes hosts to death ([[CANON]] §2); the Ripper is the waste product of a machine running so fast it
turns whole populations into ammunition.

The horror is not one Ripper. It is the arithmetic. One Ripper is easy. Fifty on a four-meter corridor
is a math problem. Four hundred on the east berm at once — because the [[CANON|Choir]] coordinates them
all and the [[Chorister]] in the tree line is still singing — is how a wall falls. Rippers do not punch
above their weight. They punch through it with their weight alone. They do not need to be clever because
they do not need to win individually. They need the operator in front of them to spend one more shot on
this body instead of on the relay behind it. That is their whole strategic value: they are expensive to
the human side and free to the Scourge, and the Scourge has more bodies than humanity has ammunition.

The Ripper is also the reason the Choir can think at all from a surface breach. The [[CANON|Choir's]]
radius is limited ([[CANON]] §6); the Scourge must mass densely for the signal to travel. The Ripper
mass is the density. Fifty Rippers in a packed advance are not just fifty bodies rushing a wall — they
are fifty signal nodes, fifty relay-hops, the substrate that lets a [[Chorister]] extend its reach and
lets a [[Cantor]] carry coordination down a lane. Kill the mass and the signal starts to thin. Kill the
relay and the mass goes blind. This is why they come together.

## Behaviour

- **Closes the distance.** Rushes the nearest living target by the most direct path — through cover,
  over dead bodies, up rubble, over its own. No flanking, no feinting, no hesitation. The
  [[CANON|Choir]] handles the thinking; the Ripper handles the running.
- **Attacks with blade forearms.** Two overhand sweeps, wide and heavy; no finesse. It does not combo,
  read spacing, or adapt. It swings until the target stops or it does.
- **Dies fast.** Low health, no meaningful armor. One clean burst drops it. The design is
  disposability: replace the dead one with three more from behind. Dying is the Ripper's final
  service to the wave.
- **Coordinated while the [[CANON|Choir]] is live.** Under Choir coverage a Ripper is part of a
  smarter whole — it closes the angle the [[Chorister]] plots, fills the gap the [[Cantor]] identifies.
  It does not feel smart. It just moves at the right time, the way traffic obeys lights that haven't
  been green yet.
- **Feral when severed.** Outside Choir range it drops to pure instinct: loudest sound, hottest heat,
  nearest movement. Still lethal, just stupid about it — it charges the wrong target, trips over the
  body in front of it, cannot exploit the flank it was already running. Easier to kill; harder to
  predict, the way purely random things are unpredictable. This is the legible proof that a severance
  worked: the mass goes dumb, right now, in front of you.
- **Effective through mass.** The damage model is overwhelm: too many entry angles to cover
  simultaneously, too many bodies to burn per reload. The Scourge never teaches the Ripper a better
  trick. It just sends more Rippers.

## Gameplay Read

- **The war's baseline pressure unit.** Not a threat to manage individually; a resource to consume.
  Kills fast, dies fast, pushes the player's economy of ammunition and time more than their tactical
  reads.
- **Scales to horror through numbers.** One Ripper in a doorway is a speed bump. Thirty in a corridor
  tell the player everything they need to know about what the Scourge is and how it fights. The art is
  tuning the number.
- **A legible signal-state indicator.** When a Ripper pack moves with Choir coordination — gaps filled,
  angles covered, the pack flinching in sync with the [[Cantor]]'s beat — it teaches why the relay is
  the actual target. When it goes feral, the sloppiness of the charge is the visible proof the
  severance worked.
- **Prioritised by proximity, not strategic value.** Players kill the Ripper because it is in their
  face. Killing it never advances the situation. This is exactly what the Scourge intends: keep the
  gun pointed at expendable bodies while the relay sits behind them. The Ripper is designed to absorb
  attention.

## Visual Motifs

- A [[Scourge-Host-Families|Rot-Infested Flesh Host]] at its rawest: bone, muscle, and ruin.
  Whatever the host was, the silhouette is forward-leaning, hunched, built entirely around reaching a
  target. No upright dignity, no readable armor, nothing the host ever chose to carry.
- Blade forearms of fused bone and black chitin — the only clean lines on the body, because the
  parasite bothered to shape the weapon properly and let everything else go to ruin. Chest cavity
  split and re-knit around parasite tissue; ribs visible through ruptured skin where the conversion
  ran fast and shallow.
- Toxic-green breach cores embedded small and dim at the sternum and collar — not the bright emitter
  nodes of a relay unit. Just anchor points, the first parasites that moved in and never moved up.
- Black chitin erupting along the spine and the back of the arms, over host bone and what is left of
  host muscle. The shoulders are the last place the original anatomy is legible. By the forearms there
  is nothing recognizably animal anymore — only the weapon the Scourge decided to grow there.
- Wet. Everything is wet: exposed sinew, ruptured tissue, the ragged seams at every joint where chitin
  meets organic flesh and the body has not finished deciding which it is.

## Sprite Brief

- Views: front, side, back.
- Hunched forward silhouette in all views — low center of gravity, head down, blade forearms extended.
  Should read at a glance as *charging*, not standing.
- Must be readable at small scale in [[Deadlane]]'s lane-top camera, at close FPS range in
  [[Scourge-Survivors]], and as a side-on target in [[Redline]]. The blade forearms are the
  orientation key in every view.
- Dying state: collapsed, blades out and slack, chitin cracked, host tissue exposed — the Scourge's
  cheapest work, legible as cheapest.
- Feral state (optional variant): same silhouette, head raised, tracking something on sound rather
  than sprinting to a coordinate — a slightly loosened posture that marks a severed unit for players
  who know to read it.

forward silhouette built entirely around a charge, blade forearms of fused bone and black chitin the
only clean lines on the body, ribs visible through ruptured skin where the conversion ran fast, small

## Appears In

- [[Scourge-Survivors]] — the pressure layer the whole game runs on: the bodies between the player
  and every relay, node, and breach organ worth killing. Without the Ripper flood the [[Chorister]]
  is exposed; without the Chorister the flood is just traffic. They are the economy of the war made
  physical and close.
- [[Deadlane]] — the base wave unit, the thing the wall was built to slow. Tower range, kill
  corridors, and wall temperature are all measured in Rippers per minute. The [[The-Wardens|Wardens]]
  do not count by kills; they count by time purchased between waves.
- [[Redline]] — the obstacle on a road that is still a road problem even after the [[Carillon]]'s
  wire is cut: a courier who severs the local signal still has to run through the bodies that were
  already committed to the route before the Choir went quiet.
