# Tell

**At a glance:** founding [[The-Listeners|Listener]] · former [[The-Wardens|Warden]] signals operator rotated off Fork duty for "hearing too clearly" · made the eleven-day record the [[The-Quiet-Rooms|Quiet Rooms]] still run on · named the practice **the Interval** — the discipline of holding the gap between hearing and answering — and is either the most careful person in the war or the most lucid-infected one, and nobody who is still in a position to check is willing to find out · hunted under standing [[The-Pyre|Pyre]] burn order; Warden tribunal open.

The name Tell came from the Fork.

On long signal duty you start calling things by what they do. The knife is a burner, the wall is a hold, the medic who gets you off the breach floor is the patch. When a [[Tuning-Fork|Fork]] goes hot on a node and everyone in the room feels the tone bore into the back of their sinuses, the one holding the tines is the Tell — the one who has found it. Standard doctrine: three shifts maximum on a live signal, then rotate, because the ones who hold it longer stop sleeping and start finishing the sentences of the dead. The Wardens rotate hard and burn out the ones who linger; that is in the field manual, not a suggestion.

Tell held a severed [[Cantor]] for eleven consecutive shifts.

The node was already cut — distance-severed, cold to the wider [[CANON|Choir]], confirmed dead to the signal by two independent Fork readings. By Warden standing orders it should have burned on day one, hour six. It did not burn. A signals operator in a sealed bay kept the dead-air compartment sealed, kept taking readings, and wrote down what changed. Three pulses before a feral pack reconnected in the dark outside the wall. Five before a [[Cantor]] could route around a kill. Nine before a captured line started ringing on its own. Not a translation. Not a conversation. A timing record, in the careful hand of someone who had been trained not to bother.

The commandant who reviewed the case noted two things in the tribunal record. Both have since been copied by hand into the operating log of every [[The-Quiet-Rooms|Quiet Room]] that follows doctrine. The first: that the Fork readings were accurate — the Wardens checked against two independent readouts, and the record held. The second: that accuracy was not the point. The point was that Tell had kept an enemy mind alive past the tactical window and listened to it, and the correct response to a kept node is fire, not a pen.

Tell was rotated off every signal assignment in the Warden corps. The [[The-Pyre|Pyre]]'s correction — a shorter document — arrived before the tribunal could finish its paperwork. Tell walked out of the hold with the timing record folded inside a sealed field case and did not come back.

That record is why there are Quiet Rooms at all.

## The Interval

Tell's name for the discipline is not "the listening" or "the study." It is **the Interval**: the deliberate gap between hearing the [[CANON|Choir]] and either acting on it or answering back. Not a pause before a decision. A held discipline — the art of keeping the gap open long enough to read what fills it, without closing it by speaking back.

You hear the signal. You hold the Interval. You write down what changes. You do not answer, because the moment you answer you have told whatever lives in that signal there is someone on the other side, and what lives in it has eaten enough worlds to know what to do with that.

Every [[The-Quiet-Rooms|Quiet Room]] runs the Interval. The burn authority pointed inward is Tell's design: if someone closes the gap, the fire goes in. A Quiet Room is not a means of communication. It is a way to keep the Interval alive long enough to read the silence between the pulses, and the only thing that makes it that rather than the other thing is the distance Tell holds between what the room contains and what it is allowed to say.

The Pyre's creed holds that a repeater kept breathing is a source not yet burned ([[CANON]] §6; [[The-Pyre]] §Creed). Tell's answer, recorded once in the margin of the original timing log and not repeated since: *the source has been burning since before the first breach. The fire hasn't worked yet.*

Whether that is analysis or something else is the question the Interval is designed not to answer.

## Role

- **Allegiance:** [[The-Listeners]], founding practitioner; no formal hierarchy, no cell structure — the order grew around Tell without ever becoming one.
- **Function:** keeper and distributor of the Quiet Room protocol; trainer of Fork-sensitives who hear clearly and have nowhere else to go; the person the Interval is named for.
- **Status:** hunted. [[The-Pyre]] burn order, standing. [[The-Wardens]] tribunal, open. Neither faction knows where Tell is except via the Quiet Room cells that run the protocol, and the cells don't say.

## The open question

The Tuning-Fork field manual says long-duty Fork operators bleed from the ears, lose sleep, and start finishing the sentences of the dead ([[Tuning-Fork]] §The cost). Tell's readings are accurate — confirmed. A clean operator with unusual sensitivity reads the same as a subtly exposed one who is managing exposure with discipline, and the difference is below what a field Fork can distinguish.

No Listener has run the Fork over Tell. The reason is the obvious one: if the reading is clean it proves only that Tell is careful, and if it is not, then the Interval itself may be the [[CANON|Choir]] learning how to wait through a human hand. The uncertainty is the cost of having the protocol at all, and Tell does not offer to remove it.

The question — uninfected scholar or lucid-infected; the Choir's careful student or its careful instrument — is open. [[The-Listeners]] do not resolve it. [[CANON]] does not lock it. The Interval holds it, the same way it holds everything else.
