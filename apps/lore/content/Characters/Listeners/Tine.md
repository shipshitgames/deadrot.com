# Tine

**At a glance:** [[The-Listeners|Listener]] founding practitioner · former [[The-Wardens|Warden]] signals officer · architect of the Quiet Room discipline; named the practice _the Audit_ · the first person to log a usable timing-mark record of a severed [[CANON|Choir]] node · hunted by both factions, protected by neither · story thread in [[War-for-the-Lanes]].

She was a Warden before she was anything else. Signals branch: she ranged repeaters, charted [[CANON|Choir]] response windows, and wrote the timing tables that three holdouts still run their drill clocks against. She was not infected. She never claimed to hear the Choir in her sleep. What she had was what her crews called an ear — she could read a [[Tuning-Fork]] pulse and tell you not just where the repeater sat but how dense the local mass was, how many paths the signal was dividing between, whether the swarm on the other side was coordinated or already starting to fray. She called it reading the room. Her commanding officers called it a targeting advantage. She started calling it something else the day she refused to file the burn order.

The target was a downed [[Chorister]] on the edge of [[The-Hollow-Lanes]], severed from the wider Choir by three walls of dead-air [[The-Switchback|Switchback]] burns. It was feral — blind, harmless to the line, already dying. The correct response was a [[Blackout-Nail]] to finish the cut and move on. Instead she stayed, with two crewmates who were either loyal or stupid or both, and listened for eleven hours on every recording rig they had. The timing marks she logged from that single session — three pulses before a feral pack reconnected, five before a [[Cantor]] could route around a burn, nine before a captured carrier line started ringing again — became the first entry in what would become the Quiet Room record. The Wardens called it targeting data. The Pyre, when they eventually saw it, called it a confession. The [[The-Listeners|Listeners]] called it the beginning of language ([[The-Quiet-Rooms]]). She filed nothing. She took the slates and she left.

The Wardens classified her as lost in action. The [[The-Pyre|Pyre]] found her once, in a basement below [[Ashgate]], and the cell that burned the room out thought they had killed her. They had not. What they burned was two weeks of records and a [[Quaver]] resonator sac she had been feeding copper-ion solution to keep its pulse audible past the normal die-off window. She rebuilt from memory and the slates she had carried on her body. She always rebuilds from the slates.

She goes by **Tine** — the Warden term for the listening prong of a [[Tuning-Fork]]. She chose it after the desertion, because it is the simplest description of what she does and what she was: the part of the instrument that touches the signal without becoming the signal. Nobody who knows her calls her anything else. She does not hold a rank. She does not run a cell in the Pyre sense or a post in the Warden sense. What she holds is **the Audit**: the Listener name for the practice of ranging a severed node over time, building its grammar, and — critically, always — never once attempting to speak back.

The Audit is the discipline she designed after the Ashgate room was burned. Its shape came from that failure: the room had been too warm, the node held too long, one crewmate left a blood-soaked wrap in the drain that gave the signal a path to a dead cable running through the wall. The lessons were structural, not spiritual, and so the rules she wrote were structural too. Sever before you study. Strip the room to ceramic and dead cable and sealed drains before the node arrives. Have a fire charge aimed inward before the organ is live enough to know it is in a room. Listen. Do not answer. Log the slates so that if the room fails the record survives in someone else's hands. These are the Room Rules ([[The-Quiet-Rooms]]) as they are now practiced anywhere Listeners have set up a stable cell. She did not intend to write a doctrine. She was trying to not die a second time and to not lose the data a second time. That they are the same discipline is, in her view, not a coincidence.

She is, by every Fork reading available, not infected. The Choir does not appear to register her as a node or a host. This is, to her, the methodological bedrock: you cannot audit a signal you are part of. The Listeners who go lucid — who let the signal in to understand it better, who argue that direct contact is the only real translation — are, in her view, contaminating the data at the source. She does not condemn them aloud. She does not work with them. The one time a lucid-contact practitioner offered to sit in on an Audit session and "provide interpretive context," she closed the room for a month and moved the node.

She is not an elder in the Pyre's sense, and she does not ordain anyone. The Listeners have no induction rite that she has designed or sanctioned. What she has instead is the reputation that precedes her through the work: if a Quiet Room has stable records, clean Room Rules, and timing marks that read like they were written by someone who expected to die in the room and arranged the notes so they would be useful afterward, Tine either built it or trained the person who did.

Whether she is right — about any of it, not just the methodology — she has no opinion she will put in writing. The slates say what the slates say. The Choir is what the Choir is. An optimizer with no off-switch ([[CANON]] §1) is not a thing you convince or defeat by understanding it better. It is a thing you build an honest accounting of, so that when someone eventually figures out what to do with that account, the account is accurate. She has been building that account for longer than most Listener crews have been alive. She intends to keep building it. Whether it turns out to matter is not a question that fits on a slate.

## Role

- **Allegiance:** [[The-Listeners]] — practitioner and de-facto founding voice, not a claimant to leadership and not a commander.
- **Function:** architect of the Quiet Room tradition ([[The-Quiet-Rooms]]); keeper of the original timing-mark record; the person both factions want neutralized for different reasons — the Pyre because she keeps Choir nodes alive and calls it data, the Wardens because she deserted with their charts and their asset classification still reads "denied."
- **Fate:** open. She has been counted dead twice and has been wrong both times. The Audit is not an institution — it is a practice kept by a small number of crews she has trained directly and a larger number who have heard her rules second-hand and try to follow them. What happens to it if she actually dies is the question none of them are ready to answer, which she considers a structural problem she has not yet solved.

## Appears In

- [[War-for-the-Lanes]] — Listener operations: she is the source of timing-mark intelligence that occasionally reaches both factions despite their standing orders; a player decision-point where the data she produced can reveal a hidden repeater faster than a standard Fork sweep, at the cost of acknowledging where it came from and what it cost to keep the room open.
- [[Deadlane]] — off-screen: the pulse-timing advantage a Warden crew uses to predict a [[Cantor]] reroute has a provenance — a set of slates handed over at a cost no field commander officially acknowledges — and that provenance is her. The data is good. Nobody says where it came from. Everybody knows.
- [[Scourge-Survivors]] — a Quiet Room found wrong inside [[Perdition]]: the records in the abandoned room are in her handwriting, organized by her system, and they end mid-entry. The Purger who finds them does not know whose handwriting it is. The player may.
