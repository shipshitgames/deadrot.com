# Vess

**At a glance:** former [[The-Wardens|Warden]] Fork-operator and the founding voice of [[The-Listeners]] · the first to transcribe the [[CANON|Choir]] as *pattern* rather than location · named the practice **the Interval** · author of the sealed Choir ledger · alive, hunted, last confirmed past [[The-Switchback]] · appears in [[War-for-the-Lanes]] / [[Deadlane]] mission thread.

The Wardens never meant to make a heretic. They meant to make a better Fork operator.

Fourteen rotations, three years, the same handed-down rig with a hairline crack in the left tine that nobody fixed because Vess swore the imperfection helped them range a [[CANON|Choir Node]] from further out. The data they handed in was clean, annotated, and on time. Every rotation. Six hours on the signal, two weeks off, back on again — the [[Tuning-Fork|rotation discipline]] held, because Vess handed it in, and the Wardens used it, and the lanes the data pointed at stayed quiet or turned into battles that were won. You do not take a [[Tuning-Fork|Fork]] off the wall when it works. You rotate the operator, watch for the signs, and rotate them back when the two weeks are up and they still look like themselves.

The sign the Wardens missed is the one the protocol was not written to catch. Not the bleeding, not the sleeplessness, not the borrowed voices — those it knows. The sign it missed was that Vess came back from each rotation *interested*. Not destabilized. Not damaged. Interested, the way an analyst is interested: writing things down after the shift, cross-referencing the previous sessions, asking for access to the archive logs of operators who had held this sector's signal before them. The Wardens gave it, because what are you going to do, deny a clean operator access to historical targeting data?

The ledger behind the targeting data is two hundred and twelve pages.

Not location. Not bearing, not radius estimate, not node classification. Pattern: spacing, repetition, call and response, the things a signal does when it is not broadcasting *at* you but *past* you. The [[CANON|Choir]]'s hive radius is a signal ([[CANON]] §5–6), and a signal, Vess wrote on page one, is a language the moment it repeats with variation. They did not write this in the official record. They wrote it in a separate log, and the log went into a kit bag, and the kit bag did not make it to any Warden archive, and that is the only reason Vess is still breathing.

The tribunal was about the missing log. The testimony was about what was in it. The tribunal asked what the signal said; Vess told them. The room went quiet in a way that is not in the official record but is in four personal logs that have since been burned. The [[The-Pyre|Pyre]]'s terms for the second hearing were not about recanting. The answer was the same both times: *the Fork found a carrier. I ran it back. This is what ran along it.*

No one in the heresy calls them the founder. Vess does not call anything. What they did was name the gap between a Fork's last pulse and the [[CANON|Choir]]'s reply — the moment when the signal is neither ranging nor silent, and something moves through it — **the Interval**. The name spread before the face did. A crew who has never met Vess and is not certain they are alive may still call a [[The-Quiet-Rooms|Quiet Room]] session *running an Interval*, and know exactly what that means: the gap, the listening, the not-yet-answered question.

The Quiet Room protocol as it exists — the sever-first rule, the no-host-budget discipline, the burn authority requirement — was largely worked out by Vess across two years of sessions that did not kill anyone and three that did. The difference was a set of rules ([[The-Quiet-Rooms|Room Rules]]) that are now the only thing keeping the heresy from being a cause of local breaches rather than a study of them.

Nobody has asked whether Vess hears the [[CANON|Choir]] without the Fork. The people who should ask are hunting them. The people who know them do not ask, because they have seen the expression Vess wears in a Quiet Room when the node pulses — attentive, not afraid, not distant — and they have decided there are some questions the protocol does not cover.

## Role

- **Allegiance:** [[The-Listeners]] — founding practitioner. Not a cell commander; not running anything. The doctrine's origin and the Listener most-wanted list's first name.
- **Function:** pattern-analysis and Choir transcription. In the field: Fork-reads, Quiet Room protocol consult, the one person the heresy's cells write to when a session produces something they cannot classify. Author of the sealed ledger the [[The-Wardens|Wardens]] have under a tribunal hold and the [[The-Pyre|Pyre]] have on a burn order and neither has actually found.
- **Fate:** alive, moving, last confirmed past [[The-Switchback]]. [[The-Hush]] will not take them; the reason is exactly what you think. Both orders are working from eighteen-month-old position data. Neither has current.

## The Interval — the practice's name

The gap between a Fork's last pulse and the Choir's reply — the moment of not-ranging, not-silent — was always there. Vess named it, and the name made it into something a crew could tell each other to find. *Run the Interval.* Keep the severed node alive, hold the dead air past the radius edge, listen into the silence the signal leaves. What the silence contains is the open question; that it contains *something* is what the two hundred and twelve pages argue. Whether Vess is right is a question neither the Pyre nor the Wardens have found a way to ask without answering it first with fire.

## Gameplay Read

- A **story / mission character** in [[War-for-the-Lanes]] — never playable. The name behind the Listener thread's coherence: [[The-Pyre|Pyre]] burn-squads are hunting one person; [[The-Wardens|Warden]] signals officers are citing one ledger; Listener cells are practicing one protocol, and they are not entirely certain its author is alive to know it.
- **Intel leverage:** a squad that locates Vess, or the ledger, or a current Interval notation holds something both factions will bargain for. The [[CANON|Choir]], if it can hear its own signal analyzed, may want the ledger stopped. The mission weight is three-sided before it starts.
- **The Listeners' living argument:** Vess does not convert anyone in dialogue. The case is made by what the ledger contains — by running a [[The-Quiet-Rooms|Quiet Room]], by watching a [[Cantor]] re-route in the exact window an Interval session predicted — and deciding whether pattern means language and language means addressability, or just means the thing is consistent the way a storm is consistent and you still cannot talk to it.

## Visual Brief

- Warden-cut field armor, worn too long without a logistics tail: ID tabs removed and the slots left bare, faction marking scorched off the shoulder plate and not replaced. A [[Tuning-Fork]] still clipped to the chest where a Warden wears rank. It is not ceremonial. It works.
- Carries the ledger in a waterproofed case patched twice — once with Pyre field tape (blood-red, obvious) and once with a scrap of Warden rating-plate (gunmetal, bolted). The two colors do not match and Vess has not fixed it.
- Scar at the left ear, lower helix: a Fork coupling ran hot on a long session and was not rotated off in time. It is not the only reason that ear is sometimes wrong about distances. The other reason is not documented.
- No Pyre-red, no Warden-grey as faction marks. The only saturated color on them is the toxic-green node glow in the Fork's throat — [[CANON|Scourge]] color held in a human hand — which is the whole picture, one way or another.
