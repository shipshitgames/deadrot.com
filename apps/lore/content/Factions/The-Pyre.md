# The Pyre

**At a glance:** faction · zealot order broken from the [[The-Wardens|Wardens]] after [[Zero-Day]] · doctrine of starve-and-sever — burn the hosts, gut the [[CANON|Choir]] repeaters from inside · cell-based, fast, expendable-by-choice DPS/infiltration · rivals not enemies of the Wardens under [[The-Pact]] · appears in [[Scourge-Survivors]] / [[Pactfall]].

Zealot operators who descend *into* breaches to **cauterize the Scourge at its source**.
Fast, expendable, scorched-earth. They believe the only cure is fire.

## Who they are
A **zealot order that broke away from the [[The-Wardens|Wardens]]** in the years after
[[Zero-Day]] (now folded into [[Starblight]]). They watched humanity "hold the line" and saw
only a slower extinction — so they made a creed of the one thing the Choir fears: getting
*close enough to burn the source*. Faith-driven, expendable by choice, organized in cells
rather than chains of command. A Purger's life is spent, gladly, to sever one more node.
Where the Wardens want to *outlast* the Scourge, the Pyre want to *make it pay for every
world.*

The split was never a mutiny over targets — both sides read [[CANON]] the same way. It was a
quarrel over patience. The Wardens believed a wall held long enough buys a generation; the
first Purgers answered that a wall is just a slower grave, and walked out the gate carrying
cautery charges instead of rangefinders. They did not take the corps's structure with them.
They took its salvage, its grief, and its dead, and built something meaner around the loss.

Against the Scourge's weakness ([[CANON]] §6) their doctrine is **starve and sever**: burn
the hosts it feeds on and destroy the **Choir repeaters** from the inside, so the local horde
goes feral.

## Command & structure
The Pyre has **no chain of command** in the Warden sense, and treats one as a liability — a
captured officer who can route a whole front is exactly the dependency the Choir is built to
exploit. Instead the order runs as **independent cells, each able to act and die without
orders from anywhere else.** Authority is local, earned, and lost the moment a cell does.

- **Cell (the burn-crew).** The working unit: a handful of Purgers built around one source-kill
  job. A cell carries its own doctrine, its own dead, and its own decision to descend. It does
  not wait for a holdout's blessing.
- **The Cinder (cell lead).** Not a rank handed down — the Purger the cell will follow *into*
  the breach, usually the one who has come back out most often. A Cinder gives the burn order
  and is expected to be first through the firebreak, not last.
- **The Ashfathers / Ashmothers (the elders).** The few who have survived enough descents to
  carry the creed instead of just spending it. They keep the rites, settle doctrine disputes
  between cells, and decide who is ready to be sworn. They command nothing in the field; they
  are owed deference, not obedience.
- **The Vow-keeper (conclave voice).** When cells must coordinate across holdouts — a shared
  front, a [[Pactfall]] reckoning, a [[The-Pact|Pact]] window — a Vow-keeper speaks for the
  order. The role is rotated and revocable, so no single voice can be turned into a lever.

Orders flow *into* a breach and stop there. A Cinder can commit a cell; nobody above can order
a cell *not* to burn a source it has chosen ([[The-Pact]] §No doctrine capture). The order
coordinates objectives. It refuses, on principle, to be a thing the Choir could ever decapitate.

## Creed & doctrine
The creed is one line, scratched on every firebreak the order has ever cut: **to hold is to
die slowly; the only cure is fire.** They do not believe the Scourge can be negotiated with,
boxed, or outlasted — [[CANON]] §1 says it is need with no off-switch, and you do not wall in
a thing that only wants *more*. You starve it and you sever it, or you lose the world on a
longer clock.

Their method is the human win condition turned into a way to die well, tied straight to
[[CANON]] §6:

- **Starve.** Deny the parasite hosts. Burn the bodies, the nests, the host-rich ground it
  feeds on before it can wear them — including, when there is no other answer, their own
  infected dead.
- **Sever.** Get *inside* the breach and kill the **Choir repeaters** from the source side, or
  push the node past the Choir's radius, so the local horde is cut from the wider mind and goes
  **feral** — blind, dumb, instinct-only. A feral horde still kills you. It just can't see you
  coming, and that is the only edge an optimizer can't model.

The doctrine is deliberately suicidal in its math: a Purger who trades one life for one severed
node has, by the creed, *won.* This is also the order's quiet refusal of the [[The-Listeners]]
heresy — a repeater kept breathing to be studied is, to the Pyre, a source not yet burned.

## Iconography
The Pyre look is locked by [[Style-Bible]] §8 — the offense grammar — and the order wears it
as scripture, not decoration. The global palette law holds: **blood on gunmetal, lit by furnace
fire.** No neon, no cool light, no clean trooper polish.

- **Insignia.** A single triangular, flame-split sigil — the same aggressive forward-leaning
  wedge as the unit silhouette — daubed in **bone-white** ([[Style-Bible]] `bone`) on scorched
  plate. It is never printed clean. It is hand-marked, re-marked over burns, and read as a tally
  of descents survived.
- **Colors.** Scorched black gunmetal and heat-baked bone-white ceramic for the body; the only
  saturated color is emissive **hellfire-orange** and **blood-hot** heat lines, plus the furnace
  glow of a visor slit ([[Style-Bible]] §8). Toxic-green is forbidden on a Purger — it belongs to
  the Scourge alone ([[CANON]] §1).
- **Materials.** Sealed, soot-blackened plates over a pressure suit; bone-ceramic panels cracked
  from repeated breach descents; ash and dried blood worked into every seam. Gear reads as
  **suicide-run breach kit** — sealed helmet, air filters, cauterizer tanks — not Warden tool-arms
  and rangefinders.
- **Heraldry & rites of mark.** The **Zealot** variant adds cloth or ash tabards and bone-white
  ritual marks (abstract, never readable text); these are honored, never mandatory. A cell brands
  its own dead-count and its severed-node tally into its plates. Variation lanes run
  **Tactical → Zealot → Perdition** ([[Style-Bible]] §8), the last being late-war armor scarred
  and breach-corrupted past repair.
- **Recognition.** You know a Purger by the furnace-slit glow before you see the wedge: a
  fanatic operator *built to die close to the source*, never a clean military trooper, never
  Warden engineering gear.

## Recruitment & culture
The Pyre does not recruit so much as **inherit the bereaved.** Most Purgers come to the order
after a wall they trusted failed — survivors of overrun holdouts, deserters who watched a line
held one second too long, civilians who carried out their own infected dead and decided the slow
way was the cruel way. The order asks one thing of them, and it is the hardest thing: be willing
to be spent.

- **The making.** A volunteer is not sworn on faith alone. They run with a cell, carry the dead,
  cut a firebreak, and are watched. To be **sworn** is to accept that your life is now a resource
  the creed may spend to sever a node — and to mean it. An Ashfather decides when, or never.
- **Daily culture.** Rites are industrial, not churchly: marking plates, sharpening cautery,
  reciting the names of the nodes a cell has severed and the Purgers it cost to do it. The order
  keeps no temples ([[The-Listeners]] have rooms; the Pyre have firebreaks). Gallows humor is
  load-bearing — a culture that expects to die laughs at it early.
- **Their own dead.** Expendability is doctrine, but it is not contempt. A Purger spent to sever
  a node is **honored, not mourned wasteful** — their tally is burned into the cell's plate so the
  count walks on. Infected dead are a different rite: burned without ceremony and without
  hesitation, because a corpse left whole is a host the Scourge has not yet been denied.
- **Civilians.** The Pyre is brutal *toward the source*, not toward the living it shields. A burn
  order put a wall between a holdout and a breach; the order remembers that. Civilians fear the
  Pyre's methods and are right to — but the firebreak is cut on their side of the line, and
  every Purger knows it.

## Playstyle
Aggressive, high-risk DPS / infiltration. → [[Scourge-Survivors]] (FPS).

## Rivalry (not war)
It is a **rivalry, not a war.** The Pyre call [[The-Wardens]] cowards who prolong the rot by
merely holding lines — undertakers with better walls — and the Wardens call the Pyre wasteful
fanatics. None of that survives a siren. **The feud stops the instant a breach opens** ([[The-Pact]];
[[Premise]] §The Pact): humans always stand together against the Scourge, no field vendetta, no
doctrine capture. When the wound opens, the argument ends, and the Pyre go through the firebreak
the Warden line holds open.

The grievance is real, so it has somewhere to go that is *not* the field. It is settled only in
sanctioned arenas — [[Pactfall]] — where the two human answers can bleed each other under lights
without ever leaving a gate unopened. The arena is pressure relief, not hypocrisy. The Pyre have
never made open war on the Wardens, and the creed forbids it: a Purger who turns a burn on
another human while the Choir is still singing has betrayed the source-kill, not served it.
