# The Wardens

**At a glance:** faction — remnant military/engineering corps · humanity's closest thing to a state ·
holds the lanes with towers, walls, and attrition · fragment-and-isolate doctrine, slow to move and
impossible to rout · reads as engineers keeping broken infrastructure alive ([[Style-Bible]] §8) ·
appears in [[Deadlane]] / [[Pactfall]].

Engineers and gunners who **hold the lanes** against the [[Scourge]] with towers, walls, and
attrition. Methodical, patient — survival over glory.

## Who they are
The **remnant military and engineering corps** — what survived of the old-world armies after the
fall ([[Zero-Day]], now the first-contact last stand inside [[Starblight]]). They kept the chain of
command, the hardware, and the doctrine, and reorganized into the closest thing humanity still has
to a **state**: hierarchical, disciplined, slow to move and impossible to rout. They believe the
species survives by *outlasting* the Scourge, not charging it. To the Wardens, every wall held is a
generation bought.

They are not optimists. They have read the same [[CANON]] the Pyre have — the Scourge never truly
dies, only moves on — and decided that *time* is the only thing worth fighting for. A holdout that
stands one more winter is a win. A lane that never opens is a victory no one will ever write down.
The Wardens are fine with that. Glory is for people who expect to be remembered.

## Command & structure
The Wardens kept the old-world **chain of command**, and it is the single thing that most separates
them from the [[The-Pyre|Pyre]]'s autonomous cells. Where a Pyre cell answers to its own conscience
and the breach in front of it, a Warden answers *up the line*.

- **The Holdfast / garrison command.** Each fortified holdout is run by a standing garrison under a
  ranking officer — a **Warden-Marshal** or holdfast commander — who owns the walls, the lane map,
  and the extraction clock. Orders flow down; they are not improvised at the wall.
- **Lane officers.** Below the Marshal, each defended corridor has an officer who allocates towers,
  fields, ammunition, and dead-road priority. This is the rank that signs off on what the [[The-Pact]]
  calls the *extraction clock* — when to hold, when to fall back, who covers the gate.
- **Crews and trades, not warriors.** The rank-and-file are organized by *trade*, not glory:
  engineers who build, gunners who hold, wallwrights who seal, couriers who carry the order between
  holdouts when the lanes go dark. A Warden's worth is their function on the line.
- **The order is sacred.** Because they coordinate by message and not by zeal, the **written order**
  — the hold order, the fall-back order, the extraction window — is treated almost as a relic. A
  [[Warden-Courier]] who delivers it intact has done a holy thing; a line that ignores it has
  already lost.

The cost of all this is the thing the Pyre mock them for: a Warden line waits for orders. That
discipline is what nearly cost a holdout its walls in the moment that birthed [[The-Pact]] — guns
sitting silent for a command while burners sprinted past them. The Wardens did not unlearn the
lesson. They wrote the Pact instead.

## Creed & doctrine
The creed is short, and every Warden can recite it: **outlast it. Hold the line. Every wall held is
a generation bought.** They do not expect to kill the Scourge — [[CANON]] §1–2 makes that
impossible — only to deny it ground for one more day, and trust that a thousand more days is the
same as winning.

Against the Scourge's one real weakness ([[CANON]] §6 — isolation and starvation) their method is
**fragment and isolate**: hold the chokepoints to break the swarm's density and cut it off from
**[[CANON|the Choir]]**, so what gets through arrives feral. The doctrine maps cleanly onto the
canon weakness:

- **Fragment.** A wall and a chokepoint force the horde to mass thin where the Wardens choose. Break
  its density and you break the connection it needs to stay coordinated.
- **Isolate.** Range and break the **repeaters/relays**, or push a node past the Choir's radius, and
  that part of the Scourge is severed — still lethal, but **blind, dumb, instinct-only**. What
  reaches the wall after that arrives *feral*, and a feral thing dies on a planted gun.
- **Starve.** Deny it hosts. Hold the civilians behind the wall, keep the dead off the open ground,
  and the front withers because it has nothing left to wear.

The Pyre burn the source from the inside; the Wardens box it out from the line. Same canon, opposite
hand — which is the whole ugly hinge of [[The-Pact]].

## Iconography
A Warden is recognized the way you recognize working infrastructure: by the wear, not the shine. The
look is locked in [[Style-Bible]] §8 and must not drift toward paladin, knight, or clean-white
sci-fi soldier — the read is **an engineer keeping broken infrastructure alive**.

- **Insignia.** A **square / buttressed shield-and-tower sigil**, stencilled in `bone`/off-white on
  gunmetal — the same square, planted, modular shape language as their armor and their towers. No
  flame, no blade; a Pyre sigil is triangular and aggressive, a Warden's is square and grounded
  ([[Style-Bible]] §8, §13.9).
- **Colors & palette.** Strictly within the global palette law — **blood on gunmetal, lit by furnace
  fire.** Bodies and plate are worn steel and `gunmetal`; marks and stencils are `bone`. The one
  permitted accent is **`industrial-yellow` hazard striping, used sparingly** — the only place a
  Warden breaks the near-monochrome warm grade. **No neon, no cyan, no cool-blue;** toxic-green is
  Scourge-only and never appears on a Warden ([[Style-Bible]] §7–8).
- **Materials & surface.** Worn steel and `gunmetal` slab armor, modular and reinforced, carrying
  **repair-welds, attrition damage, and hazard stripes** — kept alive, not new. Light is ember-lit
  on the shield and tool hardware, never a clean spotlight.
- **Banners & sigils in the field.** Holdouts mark a held lane with the shield-tower stencil sprayed
  on the wall, hand-painted and re-painted over old burns. A banner is just another surface to
  weld a stencil onto. There is nothing ornamental — no jewels, no filigree; the brand exists to be
  read at a glance and repaired in the dark.
- **How a member is recognized.** Square, planted silhouette; tool arms, power packs, welders,
  rangefinders, deployable wall anchors — gear that reads as **building & holding**, not killing
  close. A Warden looks like the wall they tend.

## Recruitment & culture
The Wardens do not call for volunteers the way the [[The-Pyre|Pyre]] do. They **absorb**. A holdout
that comes under a Warden garrison's protection comes under its discipline; its survivors are
counted, assigned a trade, and put to work on the line. You join the Wardens mostly by being alive
inside their walls when the order comes down.

- **Who joins, and why.** People who want to live more than they want to be remembered. Old-world
  soldiers who still trust a chain of command, engineers who would rather fix a wall than charge a
  breach, families who chose a roof and a ration over a martyr's death. The Pyre call them
  undertakers with better walls. The Wardens take it as a compliment — undertakers, at least, keep
  showing up.
- **How they are made.** Through *trade*, not initiation. A recruit is apprenticed to a crew —
  engineer, gunner, wallwright, courier — and earns standing by keeping their piece of the line
  alive. Rank comes from reliability under fire, not zeal. The culture rewards the gunner who never
  moves and the [[Field-Engineer]] who gets a tower back up before the next wave, not the hero who
  dies well.
- **Daily culture & rites.** Life is a duty roster: wall-watch, repair shift, drill, the reading of
  the day's orders. Their one real rite is **the order itself** — the hold order read aloud at the
  wall, acknowledged down the line, obeyed. A Warden holdout runs on the quiet faith that if
  everyone does their job, the wall stands.
- **Their dead, and expendability.** The Wardens spend lives carefully, because their whole creed is
  that lives are the resource they are buying time *for*. They will trade a position, never a
  generation. They bury their dead behind the wall when there is a wall to bury them behind, and
  they record the name — unlike the Pyre, who spend themselves on purpose and expect to be ash. A
  Warden death is an accounting error, not a sacrament.
- **Civilians.** Civilians are the *point*. Every non-combatant behind the wall is the generation
  being bought. The Wardens shelter, ration, and conscript them — and the [[Warden-Courier]] who
  runs a hold order through the rot is, in the end, running it for them.

## Playstyle
Defensive / economic / building. → [[Deadlane]] (tower defense).

## Rivalry (not war)
This is a **rivalry, not a war.** The Wardens see [[The-Pyre]] as reckless fanatics who waste lives
on suicide runs into breaches that just reopen — yet they hold the line *for* them every time the
Scourge surges (see [[Premise]] §The Pact). The feud **stops the instant a breach opens**: under
[[The-Pact]], any active breach suspends Pyre–Warden hostilities until the local front is severed,
starved, or evacuated. No field vendetta. No doctrine capture. When the sirens fall quiet, the feud
comes back — and it is settled in the arena, never as open war: [[Pactfall]].

The Wardens' named operators, each a different face of *hold the line*:

- [[Warden-Bastion]] — arena tank/support; a shield projector who turns a duel into a fortified
  position. ([[Pactfall]])
- [[Warden-Artillerist]] — ranged artillery hero; siege math and controlled firepower, never
  reckless aggression. ([[Pactfall]])
- [[Warden-Courier]] — disciplined runner who carries a hold order through the rot and intends to
  deliver it intact. ([[Redline]])
- [[Warden-Defense-Pilot]] — defensive pilot; lane defense moved into orbit, shielding the lanes
  below. ([[Starblight]])
- [[Field-Engineer]] — baseline tower builder who turns broken ground into a defendable lane.
  ([[Deadlane]])
- [[Lane-Gunner]] — heavy ranged defender who plants and never moves, winning by attrition as the
  Scourge breaks itself on the line. ([[Deadlane]])
- [[Wallwright]] — shield-and-barricade specialist who builds the last wall before a lane becomes a
  grave. ([[Deadlane]])
