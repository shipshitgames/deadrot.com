# The Lantern

**At a glance:** the old world's orbital defense crown above first-contact Earth · command ring of **the Skywatch**, humanity's one fleet to meet the [[Scourge]] in the open sky · went dark on [[Zero-Day]] — *the night we lost the sky* · the wreck the [[The-Skyhook|Skyhook]] was spliced from · dead, held by no one since the fall · seen only in [[Starblight]]'s Zero Day memory run.

We named it for the light: a ring of guns and watch-posts strung across the dark, the brightest thing humanity ever hung over its own head, lit so that nothing could come down out of the void without crossing it first. The Skywatch crewed it — every hull, every long-gun, every pair of eyes the old world could put in orbit, one fleet, no rivals yet, no Pact yet, because there was nothing yet to argue about.

Then first contact, and the Lantern was the first thing the Scourge put out. The Skywatch burned over it until there was no Skywatch, and the void-fall came down through the gap the dark left — straight onto [[Cradle-Field]] and the world under it. *The night we lost the sky* is, precisely, the night the Lantern went out.

No one ever retook it whole. Years on, the survivors cut one battered segment loose, re-crewed it, and strung the [[The-Skyhook|Skyhook]] from its bones — so the line humanity still holds in orbit is a splice of the ring it lost first. The rest of the Lantern still turns up there, unlit, drifting through the same sky the Skyhook fights to hold: a dead crown the living one was salvaged from.

## Role in the war
Not a front anymore — a wound, and a lesson. The Lantern is the sky humanity actually lost, the proof that massing everything in one bright place is exactly what an optimizer wants ([[CANON]] §6): one ring, one Skywatch, one night, and it was over. Everything after it — the dispersed holdouts, the dark discipline of [[The-Hush]], the breakable [[The-Spire-Causeway|causeway]], the Skyhook fought *piecemeal* instead of massed — is humanity learning never to build a single Lantern again. It holds no ground because it is not held; it earns its place on the map by being the reason the map looks the way it does.
